About the Foundation

Why Shivaksha Foundation exists

Much of the world's knowledge has been carried in traditions that modern research rarely studies with care. Some of it is idealised, some is dismissed, and much of it is simply undocumented. Shivaksha Foundation was established to close that gap: to study knowledge traditions, society and learning with the rigour expected of any field, and to train researchers who can do the same.

Vision

Mission

To conduct and support interdisciplinary research on knowledge systems, society and learning; to build research capacity through training and mentorship; and to share findings openly and responsibly.

Values

What we hold ourselves to

Integrity

We report what we find, including what does not work.

Rigour

Methods are chosen for the question, and limitations are stated.

Respect for knowledge holders

Communities and custodians are partners, not sources.

Openness

We share findings, methods and resources as widely as consent and rights allow.

Public benefit

Research should leave the people it involves better informed and better served.

People

Dr. Sundeep Katevarapu

Founder

Dr. Katevarapu is a clinical forensic neuropsychologist and Board Certified Behavior Analyst, and holds a Psy.D. and a Ph.D. Since 2015 he has founded and led initiatives in research, scholarly publishing, academic conferences and professional training, with a long-standing interest in bringing knowledge traditions into conversation with contemporary behavioural science.

Founder's message

When I began working at the meeting point of behavioural science and traditional knowledge, I found two kinds of writing. One celebrated traditions without examining them. The other ignored them altogether. Neither served students, scholars or the communities who hold that knowledge.

Shivaksha Foundation exists to do the harder and more useful thing: to study knowledge traditions, society and learning with clear questions, sound methods and honest reporting, and to help a new generation of researchers do the same. If you share that commitment, I invite you to work with us.

Research

How knowledge is created, kept and shared

Our research asks how knowledge is created, kept and shared, and what it means for how people learn and live today. We work across disciplines, in partnership with universities and communities, and we hold every project to the same standards of method and ethics.

I

Knowledge Systems and Contemporary Research

We study Indian and other indigenous knowledge traditions as subjects of serious scholarship: their texts, practices, institutions and ideas, examined with historical, philological, empirical and comparative methods.

Questions we explore
  • How were knowledge traditions taught, preserved and transmitted, and what can contemporary education learn from those systems?
  • What do classical ideas about mind, learning and community offer present-day research, and where do they fall short?
  • How can traditional knowledge be documented ethically, with credit and consent for its custodians?
II

Society, Culture and Learning

We support research on how people learn, live together and change: education, culture, community development and the social conditions of well-being.

Questions we explore
  • What makes teaching effective in multilingual, low-resource classrooms?
  • How do communities sustain cultural knowledge across generations?
  • What evidence do teachers, local institutions and civil society need most, and in what form will they use it?
III

Research Capacity and Scholarly Integrity

We help researchers do better research through methods training, mentorship, research ethics and responsible publishing.

  • Workshops on qualitative, quantitative, textual and mixed methods.
  • Writing and peer-review mentorship for doctoral and early-career researchers.
  • Practical guidance on research ethics, consent, authorship and data management.
  • Open resources: reading lists, method guides and templates, published with attribution.

Our work connects to the UN Sustainable Development Goals 4 (quality education), 10 (reduced inequalities), 11.4 (safeguarding cultural heritage) and 17 (partnerships for the goals).

Research principles

The standard every project meets

Clear research questions and methods suited to them.

Ethical review and informed consent wherever people or community knowledge are involved.

Credit, consent and benefit-sharing for communities and custodians of traditional knowledge.

Respect for privacy, confidentiality and data protection.

Transparent reporting of limitations, funding and conflicts of interest.

Responsible authorship, citation and dissemination, with plain communication that does not overstate findings.

Partnership process

How institutional partnerships work

  1. Send an enquiry with a short institutional profile and proposed area of collaboration.
  2. We review fit with our pillars, feasibility and mutual value.
  3. A discussion is arranged with the relevant people.

Frequently Asked Questions

About Shivaksha Foundation

A few answers about our work, opportunities and independence.

01

What does the Foundation do?

We research knowledge traditions, society and learning, and help researchers build their skills through training and mentorship.

02

Is the Foundation a religious organisation?

No. We study knowledge traditions as scholarship. We do not promote any religion, and our research follows the same ethical and methodological standards as any academic field.

03

Is it part of another organisation?

No. Shivaksha Foundation is an independent legal entity with its own board and accounts. Any collaboration with other organisations is made under a written agreement and listed on our Governance page.

04

Can I collaborate on a project?

Concept-note guidance is on the Research page. Its form is not currently connected to a submission service, so entries are not sent or reviewed.

05

Do you offer internships?

When an internship is open, it appears on the Get Involved page with eligibility, duration, supervision and whether it is paid. Check that page for current openings.

06

Are opportunities paid?

Each notice states clearly whether a stipend, honorarium, fee or reimbursement applies.

07

Can I donate? Are tax benefits available?

We are not accepting donations at present, and no tax benefit is available for contributions. We will update this answer if and when approvals are granted.

08

Do you publish a journal?

Not yet. A journal is on our Roadmap and will launch only when its editorial board, peer-review process and policies are in place.
